📒Meeting Outline

Lumi Migration Overview and Self-Migration Process

• Context and Objective  
  The meeting focused on the migration of SOA tables from Cornerstone to Lumi, specifically for tables opting for self-migration. Cornerstone is scheduled for decommission by the end of the year, necessitating migration of all data and use cases to Lumi. Two migration approaches exist: Ascend (central team-led) and Self-Migrate (table owners lead migration). Approximately 340 tables have chosen Self-Migrate.

• Current vs. Target Data Flow  
  Currently, data sources ingest into Cornerstone, which replicates data to Lumi. The target state is direct ingestion from sources into Lumi, eliminating Cornerstone replication. Self-Migrate owners are responsible for setting up direct Lumi ingestion pipelines.

• Self-Migrate Workflow  
  The migration process is divided into six key phases:  
  1. Setup Preparation: Ensure tables are replicated from Cornerstone to Lumi to enable validation. Confirm Lumi capability readiness for each table; a readiness list will be shared post-meeting.  
  2. Pipeline Build: Table owners must create Lumi-first feeds, including metadata setup and validation on the Lumi portal, configuring file transfer protocols, and performing sample ingestions in environments E1, E2, and E3.  
  3. Cut Over Preparation: Confirm no downstream dependencies on Cornerstone tables, and metadata on Cornerstone remains unchanged during Lumi setup. Metadata changes on Cornerstone will be frozen after June 30.  
  4. Data Validation: Use the DVT tool to compare data between replicated and Lumi-first tables over five days to ensure 100% data match.  
  5. Cut Over Execution: Submit an incident with business and technical owner approvals to initiate cut over. The platform team will execute the cut over, disabling Cornerstone replication.  
  6. Post Cut Over: Seven days after cut over, the Cornerstone table is soft decommissioned (inaccessible, ingestion stopped). Thirty days later, data deletion from Cornerstone occurs.

• Roles and Responsibilities  
  • Table owners are responsible for pipeline setup, metadata validations, and raising incidents for cut over.  
  • Platform team supports capability readiness, cut over execution, and incident handling via EDPRM with standard SLAs.  
  • Owners must ensure no downstream dependencies before cut over but can start pipeline setup even if not cut over ready.

• Recommendations and Timeline  
  Owners are encouraged to start pipeline setup immediately, aiming to complete E1 and E2 environment setups by next month to avoid last-minute delays. Waiting until the September 30 deadline is discouraged due to the complexity and time required.

Q&A and Clarifications

• Derived Tables and Use Case Queries  
  Questions about derived tables ingested via ODL (Output Data Layer) and code deployment differences (SPT vs. Gradle) were raised. The session clarified it focuses on SOR (Source of Record) tables directly ingested from source systems, not use case outputs. Offline discussions were suggested for use case-specific queries.

• Cut Over Process Details  
  Cut over involves switching clone tables (with _clone suffix) to main tables and disabling Cornerstone replication. Cornerstone tables remain accessible for seven days post cut over before soft decommissioning. Cut over is performed simultaneously across environments E1, E2, and E3 with a single incident request.

• Metadata Changes and Synchronization  
  Metadata between Cornerstone and Lumi must remain synchronized during migration to avoid cut over failures. Schema changes on Cornerstone are restricted and require approvals. Differences in timestamp column data types between platforms were noted but not expected to cause issues if Lumi metadata matches replicated tables.

• Handling Decommissioned Columns  
  Columns marked as "is published = false" in Cornerstone (not physically deleted) still appear in replicated tables. The recommendation is to align Lumi tables with replicated tables for smooth cut over. Offline discussions were proposed for specific cases involving column removal and downstream impact.

• Cut Over SLAs  
  The cut over execution itself takes approximately 1-2 hours. The overall timeline from raising the incident to execution depends on queue and readiness but typically aims for completion within a week. Multiple cut overs are often handled in a single call.

• Downstream Dependency Management  
  Table owners must verify and communicate downstream dependencies before cut over. The platform team performs sanity checks on cut over requests to confirm no active downstream usage. A central tracker is maintained for dependencies and migration plans.

• Approval Requirements for Cut Over  
  Business and technical owner approvals must be attached to the cut over incident request.

• No Exceptions for Post Cut Over Cornerstone Table Retention  
  The process does not allow exceptions to keep Cornerstone tables active after cut over to avoid data inconsistencies and confusion.

• Upcoming Automation for Decommissioning  
  A decommission utility will be introduced around mid-June to automate Cornerstone table soft decommissioning seven days after cut over.

• Next Steps Communication  
  The platform team will share the presentation deck and send outreach emails to table owners requesting planned migration dates. Business teams are requested to coordinate with their PU partners to confirm migration timelines.
